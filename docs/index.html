      How often do big ones happen here?

      For each region, magnitudes follow the Gutenberg-Richter law: every step up in magnitude is about ten times rarer. Fitting the b-value by maximum likelihood gives each region's expected rate of large events. A Poisson model then turns that rate into the chance of at least one in the next 30 days.

      From feed to page

      1. USGS feedRolling 7-day GeoJSON every hour. A one-year FDSN backfill runs on the first run.
      2. BronzeRaw records landed as date-partitioned Parquet, untouched, so any day can be replayed.
      3. SilverValidated, typed, de-duplicated DuckDB table. Idempotent upserts keep the latest USGS revision. Bad rows go to quarantine.
      4. Golddbt models and data tests: staging view, daily facts, region marts and frequency-magnitude tables.
      5. AnalyticsGutenberg-Richter fits and DBSCAN sequence detection.
      6. ServeApache Airflow orchestrates locally. GitHub Actions runs the same tasks serverless every hour and deploys this page.
      Data: U.S. Geological Survey Earthquake Hazards Program. Probabilities are statistical illustrations, not official forecasts.
